# Common Crop Diseases — Agricultural Knowledge Base

## Rice Blast (Magnaporthe oryzae)

### Symptoms
- Diamond-shaped or elliptical lesions on leaves with gray or white centers and dark brown borders
- Lesions on nodes causing node blast and stem breakage
- Panicle neck blast causing white empty grains
- Lesions may coalesce, killing the entire leaf

### Favorable Conditions
- High humidity (>90%) and temperatures between 25-28°C
- Prolonged leaf wetness
- Excessive nitrogen fertilization
- Close plant spacing

### Organic Treatment
- Neem oil spray (3-5 ml per liter of water)
- Trichoderma viride seed treatment (4g per kg seed)
- Pseudomonas fluorescens foliar spray
- Silicon-rich amendments (rice husk ash)

### Chemical Treatment
- Tricyclazole 75% WP (0.6 g/L water) — preventive spray
- Isoprothiolane 40% EC (1.5 ml/L) — curative spray
- Carbendazim 50% WP (1 g/L) — systemic fungicide
- Azoxystrobin 23% SC (1 ml/L) — broad-spectrum

### Prevention
- Use resistant varieties (e.g., CO 51, IR 64)
- Balanced fertilization — avoid excess nitrogen
- Maintain proper plant spacing
- Drain fields periodically to reduce humidity
- Remove crop residues after harvest

## Wheat Rust (Puccinia species)

### Types
- **Stem Rust (Black Rust)** — Puccinia graminis
- **Leaf Rust (Brown Rust)** — Puccinia triticina
- **Stripe Rust (Yellow Rust)** — Puccinia striiformis

### Symptoms
- **Stem Rust**: Dark reddish-brown pustules on stems and leaf sheaths
- **Leaf Rust**: Small, round, orange-brown pustules scattered on upper leaf surface
- **Stripe Rust**: Yellow-orange pustules arranged in stripes along leaf veins
- Severe infections cause premature leaf drying and shriveled grains

### Favorable Conditions
- Cool nights (10-15°C for stripe rust) or warm days (20-30°C for stem rust)
- High humidity and morning dew
- Dense crop canopy
- Late-sown crops

### Organic Treatment
- Sulfur-based sprays (3g/L) — preventive
- Neem oil (5 ml/L) — early intervention
- Compost tea foliar spray for mild infections

### Chemical Treatment
- Propiconazole 25% EC (1 ml/L) — highly effective
- Tebuconazole 25.9% EC (1 ml/L) — systemic action
- Mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g/L) — contact fungicide
- Azoxystrobin + Cyproconazole (1 ml/L) — combination

### Prevention
- Grow resistant varieties
- Timely sowing (avoid late sowing)
- Remove volunteer wheat and alternate hosts (barberry)
- Monitor fields regularly from tillering stage

## Tomato Early Blight (Alternaria solani)

### Symptoms
- Dark brown to black concentric rings forming target-shaped spots on lower leaves
- Lesions start on oldest leaves and progress upward
- Yellowing around lesions
- Stem lesions appearing as dark, sunken cankers
- Fruit lesions near stem end with leathery, dark sunken areas

### Favorable Conditions
- Temperatures between 24-29°C
- Alternating wet and dry periods
- Overhead irrigation
- Nutrient-stressed plants (especially low nitrogen and potassium)

### Organic Treatment
- Copper-based fungicides (Bordeaux mixture 1%)
- Bacillus subtilis biofungicide spray
- Neem oil (3 ml/L water)
- Baking soda spray (1 tablespoon per gallon of water) for mild cases

### Chemical Treatment
- Chlorothalonil 75% WP (2 g/L) — preventive
- Mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g/L) — contact fungicide
- Azoxystrobin 23% SC (1 ml/L) — systemic
- Difenoconazole 25% EC (0.5 ml/L) — curative

### Prevention
- Crop rotation (3-year cycle, avoid other Solanaceae)
- Mulching to prevent soil splash
- Adequate spacing for air circulation
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris
- Stake plants to keep foliage off ground
- Water at soil level, avoid wetting foliage

## Tomato Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)

### Symptoms
- Water-soaked, pale green to dark brown lesions on leaves
- White fuzzy mold growth on undersides of leaves in humid conditions
- Rapid browning and death of leaves and stems
- Firm, dark brown, greasy lesions on fruits
- Can destroy entire crop within days in favorable conditions

### Favorable Conditions
- Cool temperatures (15-22°C)
- High humidity (>90%) and prolonged leaf wetness
- Foggy or misty weather
- Dense plantings

### Organic Treatment
- Copper hydroxide spray (2 g/L)
- Bordeaux mixture (1%)
- Bacillus subtilis
- Remove and burn infected plants immediately

### Chemical Treatment
- Metalaxyl + Mancozeb (2.5 g/L) — systemic + contact
- Cymoxanil + Mancozeb (3 g/L)
- Dimethomorph 50% WP (1 g/L) — curative
- Chlorothalonil 75% WP (2 g/L) — preventive

### Prevention
- Plant resistant varieties
- Ensure good air circulation
- Avoid overhead irrigation
- Remove volunteer plants and crop debris
- Scout fields frequently during cool, wet weather

## Potato Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)

### Symptoms
- Water-soaked lesions on leaf tips and margins
- Dark brown to purple-black lesions that expand rapidly
- White sporulation on leaf undersides
- Infected tubers show reddish-brown dry rot extending inward
- Foul smell when secondary bacterial infection occurs

### Favorable Conditions
- Cool temperatures (10-20°C) with high humidity
- Prolonged wet weather
- Morning fog and dew

### Organic Treatment
- Copper oxychloride (3 g/L)
- Bordeaux mixture spray
- Bio-agent Trichoderma harzianum

### Chemical Treatment
- Mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g/L) — preventive schedule
- Metalaxyl-M + Mancozeb (2.5 g/L)
- Fenamidone + Mancozeb (3 g/L)
- Cymoxanil + Mancozeb (3 g/L)

### Prevention
- Use certified disease-free seed tubers
- Plant resistant varieties
- Hill up soil around stems to protect tubers
- Destroy infected plant material
- Proper storage with ventilation

## Cotton Bollworm (Helicoverpa armigera)

### Symptoms
- Circular bore holes in cotton bolls
- Larvae feeding inside bolls, destroying lint and seeds
- Frass (excrement) visible at boll entry points
- Damaged squares and flowers dropping prematurely
- Young larvae may feed on leaves and tender shoots

### Identification
- Eggs: Tiny, spherical, white to yellowish, laid singly on plant parts
- Larvae: Up to 40mm, color varies from green to brown with dark stripes
- Moths: Brownish-yellow forewings with a dark spot

### Organic Treatment
- Neem seed kernel extract (NSKE 5%)
- Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) spray (2 g/L)
- Trichogramma parasitoid release (1.5 lakh eggs per hectare)
- NPV (Nuclear Polyhedrosis Virus) spray (250 LE/ha)
- Hand-picking larvae in small fields

### Chemical Treatment
- Emamectin benzoate 5% SG (0.4 g/L)
- Spinosad 45% SC (0.3 ml/L)
- Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC (0.3 ml/L)
- Indoxacarb 15.8% EC (0.5 ml/L)

### Prevention
- Plant Bt cotton varieties where approved
- Install pheromone traps (5 per hectare)
- Maintain trap crops (pigeon pea, marigold)
- Bird perches to attract insectivorous birds
- Deep summer plowing to destroy pupae

## Maize Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda)

### Symptoms
- Irregular holes in leaves with ragged edges
- Larvae feed inside the whorl, producing large amounts of frass
- Windowpane-like feeding damage on young leaves
- Destruction of growing point in severe cases
- Ear damage with larvae boring into cobs

### Identification
- Larvae: Light brown to dark green, up to 35mm, inverted Y-shape on head
- Moths: Grayish-brown, 30-35mm wingspan
- Egg masses: 100-200 eggs covered in felt-like scales

### Organic Treatment
- Neem oil spray (5 ml/L)
- Bacillus thuringiensis var. kurstaki (2 g/L)
- Metarhizium rileyi (fungal biocontrol, 2 × 10⁸ spores/ml)
- Sand + lime mixture into whorl

### Chemical Treatment
- Emamectin benzoate 5% SG (0.4 g/L)
- Spinetoram 11.7% SC (0.5 ml/L)
- Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC (0.3 ml/L)
- Thiamethoxam 12.6% + Lambda-cyhalothrin 9.5% ZC (0.5 ml/L)

### Prevention
- Early planting to escape peak pest period
- Intercropping with legumes (beans, cowpea)
- Install pheromone traps for monitoring
- Maintain field sanitation
- Encourage natural enemies (parasitoids, predators)

## Powdery Mildew (Various crops)

### Affected Crops
Wheat, barley, cucurbits (squash, cucumber, melon), grapes, roses, mango

### Symptoms
- White to grayish powdery coating on leaves, stems, and buds
- Starts as small white spots, spreads to cover large areas
- Leaves may curl, distort, or turn yellow
- Premature leaf drop
- Reduced fruit quality and size

### Favorable Conditions
- Dry weather with moderate temperatures (20-25°C)
- High humidity (but NOT rain — rain washes off spores)
- Poor air circulation
- Shaded areas

### Organic Treatment
- Sulfur dust or wettable sulfur (3 g/L)
- Potassium bicarbonate spray (3 g/L)
- Milk spray (10% dilution)
- Neem oil (3 ml/L)

### Chemical Treatment
- Hexaconazole 5% EC (2 ml/L)
- Myclobutanil 10% WP (1 g/L)
- Azoxystrobin 23% SC (1 ml/L)
- Sulfur 80% WP (3 g/L)

### Prevention
- Choose resistant varieties
- Proper plant spacing
- Prune for air circulation
- Avoid excessive nitrogen
- Remove infected plant parts promptly

## Downy Mildew (Various crops)

### Affected Crops
Grapes, cucurbits, onion, lettuce, pearl millet

### Symptoms
- Yellowish-green spots on upper leaf surface
- Grayish-purple fuzzy growth on leaf undersides
- Leaves curl, brown, and die
- In grapes: "oil spot" lesions on leaves
- In onion: pale green elongated patches, leaves collapse

### Favorable Conditions
- Cool, moist conditions (15-20°C)
- High humidity, dew, fog
- Overcrowded plantings

### Organic Treatment
- Copper-based fungicides (Bordeaux mixture 1%)
- Copper hydroxide (2 g/L)
- Potassium phosphonate

### Chemical Treatment
- Metalaxyl + Mancozeb (2.5 g/L)
- Fosetyl-Al 80% WP (2 g/L)
- Dimethomorph 50% WP (1 g/L)
- Azoxystrobin 23% SC (1 ml/L)

### Prevention
- Use resistant varieties
- Good drainage and air circulation
- Avoid overhead irrigation
- Remove crop debris
- Crop rotation

## Bacterial Wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum)

### Affected Crops
Tomato, potato, eggplant, pepper, banana, ginger

### Symptoms
- Sudden wilting of the entire plant without yellowing
- Lower leaves wilt first, entire plant follows within days
- Brown discoloration of vascular tissue (cut stem shows brown ring)
- Bacterial ooze: cut stem placed in water produces milky white streaming
- Plants may appear to recover at night but wilt again in daytime

### Favorable Conditions
- Warm temperatures (25-35°C)
- High soil moisture
- Acidic soils
- Wounds from nematodes or cultivation

### Organic Treatment
- No effective cure once infected — remove and destroy plants
- Trichoderma viride soil application (preventive)
- Pseudomonas fluorescens root dip before transplanting
- Lime application to raise soil pH above 7.0

### Chemical Treatment
- No effective chemical cure available
- Soil drenching with copper oxychloride (3 g/L) — partial suppression
- Streptocycline (0.5 g/L) + Copper oxychloride — for bacterial management

### Prevention
- Use resistant rootstocks or varieties
- Crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops (3-4 years)
- Raise soil pH with lime
- Solarize soil before planting
- Avoid injury to roots during cultivation
- Use disease-free seedlings

## Fusarium Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum)

### Affected Crops
Tomato, banana, chickpea, cotton, watermelon

### Symptoms
- Yellowing and wilting of leaves on one side of plant
- Brown discoloration of vascular tissue
- Older leaves affected first, progressing upward
- Stunted growth and eventual plant death
- In banana: "Panama disease" causing total plant collapse

### Favorable Conditions
- Warm temperatures (24-28°C)
- Sandy, acidic soils
- Poor drainage
- Soil-borne fungus can persist for decades

### Organic Treatment
- Trichoderma harzianum soil application (2-3 kg/ha)
- Bio-agent Bacillus subtilis
- Neem cake soil amendment (200 kg/ha)
- Organic matter amendments to promote beneficial microbes

### Chemical Treatment
- Carbendazim soil drench (1 g/L)
- Thiophanate-methyl (1 g/L)
- Note: Fungicides have limited effectiveness as pathogen is soil-borne

### Prevention
- Use resistant varieties (most effective strategy)
- Soil solarization for 4-6 weeks
- Crop rotation with non-host crops
- Raise soil pH with lime
- Avoid movement of infected soil between fields

## Leaf Curl Virus (Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus)

### Affected Crops
Tomato (primary), pepper, beans

### Symptoms
- Upward curling and cupping of leaves
- Yellowing of leaf margins
- Stunted growth with reduced internodes
- Flowers drop without setting fruit
- Infected young plants may be completely non-productive

### Vector
- Transmitted by whitefly (Bemisia tabaci)

### Organic Treatment
- Yellow sticky traps to monitor and reduce whitefly population
- Neem oil spray (5 ml/L) against whitefly
- Reflective mulch to repel whitefly
- Remove and destroy infected plants

### Chemical Treatment
- Imidacloprid 17.8% SL (0.3 ml/L) — whitefly control
- Thiamethoxam 25% WG (0.3 g/L) — systemic
- Diafenthiuron 50% WP (1 g/L)
- Spiromesifen 22.9% SC (0.7 ml/L)

### Prevention
- Use virus-resistant tomato varieties
- Seedling treatment with Imidacloprid before transplanting
- Use insect-proof net nurseries
- Avoid planting near infected fields
- Remove alternative host weeds

## Healthy Plant Indicators

### How to Identify a Healthy Crop
- Uniform green color appropriate to the crop and growth stage
- No spots, lesions, or discoloration on leaves
- Strong, upright stems without cankers
- Good root development (white, firm roots)
- Normal leaf size and shape for the variety
- Active new growth at shoot tips
- Flowers and fruits developing normally
- No insect damage or pest presence
- No wilting during normal conditions

### General Crop Health Maintenance
- Regular soil testing (every 1-2 seasons)
- Balanced fertilization based on soil test results
- Proper irrigation management
- Integrated pest management practices
- Crop rotation and diversification
- Regular field scouting and monitoring
