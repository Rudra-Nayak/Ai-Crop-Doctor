# Pest Identification and Management — Agricultural Knowledge Base

## Aphids (Aphis spp., Myzus persicae)

### Affected Crops
Wheat, mustard, vegetables (cabbage, potato, tomato), pulses, cotton

### Identification
- Small (1-3mm), soft-bodied insects; green, black, yellow, or brown
- Found in colonies on shoot tips, undersides of leaves, and flower buds
- Secrete honeydew leading to sooty mold growth
- Winged and wingless forms present in the same colony

### Damage
- Suck plant sap causing leaf curling, yellowing, and stunted growth
- Transmit viral diseases (Potato Leaf Roll Virus, Turnip Mosaic Virus)
- Honeydew attracts ants and supports sooty mold growth
- Heavy infestations cause wilting and reduced yields

### Organic Management
- Strong water spray to dislodge aphids
- Neem oil spray (5 ml/L)
- Insecticidal soap spray (5 g/L)
- Release ladybird beetles (Coccinella septempunctata) — 1000/ha
- Encourage natural enemies: lacewings, parasitic wasps

### Chemical Management
- Imidacloprid 17.8% SL (0.3 ml/L)
- Thiamethoxam 25% WG (0.3 g/L)
- Dimethoate 30% EC (2 ml/L) — contact + systemic
- Acetamiprid 20% SP (0.3 g/L)

### Prevention
- Yellow sticky traps for monitoring (early warning)
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization (promotes succulent growth)
- Companion planting with marigold, garlic, or chives
- Remove alternate host weeds

## Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci)

### Affected Crops
Tomato, cotton, brinjal (eggplant), chili, beans, cucurbits

### Identification
- Tiny (1-1.5mm), white moth-like insects on leaf undersides
- Fly up in a cloud when disturbed
- Nymphs are translucent, scale-like, on leaf undersides
- Leave honeydew and sooty mold on leaves

### Damage
- Direct feeding causes leaf chlorosis, wilting, premature leaf drop
- Primary vector of devastating viral diseases:
  - Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV)
  - Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV)
  - Cassava Mosaic Virus
- Honeydew reduces photosynthesis

### Organic Management
- Yellow sticky traps (25-30 per hectare)
- Neem oil spray (5 ml/L) at 7-day intervals
- Insecticidal soap (10 g/L)
- Release Encarsia formosa parasitoid in greenhouses
- Reflective silver mulch to repel whiteflies

### Chemical Management
- Spiromesifen 22.9% SC (0.7 ml/L)
- Diafenthiuron 50% WP (1 g/L)
- Pyriproxyfen 10% EC (1 ml/L) — growth regulator
- Thiamethoxam 25% WG (0.3 g/L)

### Prevention
- Use virus-resistant varieties
- Install insect-proof nets on nurseries
- Avoid planting tomatoes near cotton or other hosts
- Remove and destroy infected plants immediately
- Crop-free period between seasons

## Stem Borer (Scirpophaga incertulas — Rice)

### Affected Crops
Rice (primary), sugarcane, maize

### Identification
- Moth: Small, straw-colored with a dark spot on forewing
- Larvae: Creamy white with brown head, bore into stems
- Egg masses: Covered with buff-colored hairs on leaf surface

### Damage
- **Dead Heart**: Central shoot dies in vegetative stage (wilts, turns brown, easily pulled out)
- **White Ear**: Panicle turns white and empty in reproductive stage
- Larvae feed inside stem, leaving frass and tunnels
- Yield losses can reach 30-70% in severe cases

### Organic Management
- Light traps to attract and capture moths (5 per hectare)
- Release Trichogramma japonicum egg parasitoid (50,000/ha, 6-8 times)
- Remove and destroy stubble after harvest
- Clip leaf tips of seedlings before transplanting to remove egg masses

### Chemical Management
- Cartap hydrochloride 4G granules (18-20 kg/ha in standing water)
- Chlorantraniliprole 0.4% GR (10 kg/ha)
- Fipronil 0.3% GR (16-18 kg/ha)
- Stem application: Monocrotophos (dissolve in water, apply at stem base)

### Prevention
- Early and synchronous planting in a community
- Avoid excessive nitrogen
- Maintain recommended plant spacing
- Use resistant varieties (where available)
- Harvest at ground level and destroy stubble

## Thrips (Thrips tabaci, Scirtothrips dorsalis)

### Affected Crops
Onion, garlic, chili, mango, grapes, cotton, groundnut

### Identification
- Very small (1-2mm), slender insects with fringed wings
- Color: pale yellow to dark brown
- Found on young leaves, flowers, and shoot tips
- Move quickly when disturbed

### Damage
- Silvery streaks or patches on leaves from cell destruction
- Leaf edges curl upward and tips turn brown
- In onion: leaves become white/silvery and twist
- In chili: leaf curling and "chili leaf curl complex"
- Fruit scarring and distortion
- Vector of Tomato Spotted Wilt Virus and Groundnut Bud Necrosis Virus

### Organic Management
- Blue sticky traps (thrips attracted to blue, unlike aphids which prefer yellow)
- Neem oil (5 ml/L) at 7-day intervals
- Spinosad 45% SC (0.3 ml/L) — derived from soil bacterium
- Beauveria bassiana (fungal biocontrol, 5 g/L)
- Inter-row mulching to reduce soil pupation

### Chemical Management
- Fipronil 5% SC (1 ml/L)
- Spinetoram 11.7% SC (0.5 ml/L)
- Diafenthiuron 50% WP (1 g/L)
- Acephate 75% SP (1 g/L)

### Prevention
- Overhead irrigation to physically dislodge thrips
- Avoid planting near onion or garlic fields
- Remove weed hosts
- Crop rotation
- Use reflective mulches

## Red Spider Mite (Tetranychus urticae)

### Affected Crops
Tomato, brinjal, cotton, tea, apple, roses, beans

### Identification
- Very tiny (0.5mm), oval, red to greenish-yellow
- Visible webbing on leaf undersides and between leaves
- Use hand lens for identification
- Eggs are spherical, translucent, on leaf undersides

### Damage
- Fine stippling/speckling on upper leaf surface
- Leaves turn bronze, then dry and fall off
- Webbing covers shoots and fruits in severe cases
- Rapid population explosion in hot, dry weather
- Yield losses up to 50% in severe infestations

### Organic Management
- Strong water spray to break webs and dislodge mites
- Neem oil (5 ml/L) at 5-day intervals
- Sulfur dust or wettable sulfur (3 g/L)
- Release predatory mite Phytoseiulus persimilis
- Stethorus beetle (mite predator) conservation

### Chemical Management
- Propargite 57% EC (2 ml/L) — specific acaricide
- Fenazaquin 10% EC (1.5 ml/L)
- Spiromesifen 22.9% SC (0.8 ml/L)
- Abamectin 1.8% EC (0.5 ml/L)
- Avoid pyrethroids — they kill natural enemies and cause mite outbreaks

### Prevention
- Maintain adequate irrigation (mites thrive in dry, dusty conditions)
- Avoid water stress
- Remove heavily infested plant parts
- Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides that kill mite predators
- Intercropping with coriander or marigold

## Fruit Fly (Bactrocera dorsalis, Bactrocera cucurbitae)

### Affected Crops
Mango, guava, citrus, cucurbits (bitter gourd, ridge gourd, pumpkin)

### Identification
- Medium-sized fly (6-8mm), dark brown/black with yellow markings
- Wings have characteristic dark bands
- Female punctures fruit skin to lay eggs inside
- Maggots (white, legless larvae) feed inside fruit

### Damage
- Puncture marks on fruit surface
- Fruit decays from inside due to larval feeding
- Secondary bacterial/fungal infections at puncture sites
- Premature fruit drop
- Up to 80% fruit loss in mango and cucurbits

### Organic Management
- Methyl eugenol traps for male annihilation (10-12 per hectare)
- Cue-lure traps for cucurbit fruit fly
- Bait spray: Malathion 50% EC (2 ml/L) + jaggery/molasses (10 g/L) — spot application
- Neem oil spray on developing fruits (3 ml/L)
- Protein bait traps
- Harvest fruits at 75% maturity to avoid peak infestation

### Chemical Management
- Bait spray technique (preferred over cover spray):
  Malathion 0.05% + protein hydrolysate 0.05%
- Cover spray: Deltamethrin 2.8% EC (0.5 ml/L) on fruit surface
- Dimethoate 30% EC (2 ml/L) — systemic

### Prevention
- Collect and destroy fallen fruits daily
- Deep plow after harvest to expose pupae
- Bag individual fruits (mango) with paper/cloth bags
- Field sanitation: remove all crop residues
- Community-level trapping program

## Nematodes (Root-knot Nematode — Meloidogyne spp.)

### Affected Crops
Tomato, brinjal, okra, carrot, potato, banana, almost all vegetables

### Identification
- Microscopic worm-like organisms in soil
- Symptoms visible on roots: swollen galls or knots
- Above-ground: stunting, yellowing, wilting despite adequate water
- Poor root development; roots appear clubbed or knotted

### Damage
- Galls on roots disrupt water and nutrient uptake
- Secondary infections by fungi and bacteria through damaged roots
- Yield losses 20-50%, up to 100% in severe infestations
- Fields become unsuitable for susceptible crops for years

### Organic Management
- Paecilomyces lilacinus (egg parasite) soil application (2 kg/ha)
- Purpureocillium lilacinum
- Neem cake (250-500 kg/ha) soil incorporation
- Marigold as trap crop or rotation crop (suppresses nematode populations)
- Apply Trichoderma + Pseudomonas at planting

### Chemical Management
- Carbofuran 3G (33 kg/ha) at planting — handle with extreme care, highly toxic
- Phorate 10G granules
- Fluopyram-based nematicides (newer, safer chemistry)

### Prevention
- Crop rotation with non-host crops (cereals, grasses)
- Soil solarization (cover with clear plastic for 4-6 weeks in summer)
- Use nematode-resistant rootstocks (tomato: Solanum torvum)
- Clean equipment between fields
- Raise seedlings in nematode-free soil mix
